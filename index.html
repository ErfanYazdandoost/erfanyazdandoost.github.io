About

Biography

Erfan Yazdandoost Hamedani is an Assistant Professor in the Department of Systems and Industrial Engineering at the University of Arizona, where he has been a faculty member since 2020 (Research Assistant Professor, 2020–2021; tenure-track Assistant Professor since August 2021). He is also an affiliate member of the Graduate Interdisciplinary Programs in Statistics and in Applied Mathematics. His research focuses on the theory and design of efficient algorithms for large-scale optimization problems arising in machine learning, statistical learning, and multi-agent systems — with particular emphasis on bilevel optimization, saddle point (minimax) problems, variational inequalities, and distributed optimization over networks.

He received his Ph.D. in Industrial Engineering and Operations Research (with a minor in Statistics) from The Pennsylvania State University in 2020 under the supervision of Prof. Necdet S. Aybat, and his B.Sc. in Mathematics and Applications from the University of Tehran. His work appears in leading optimization and machine learning venues — including SIAM Journal on Optimization, IEEE Transactions on Automatic Control, INFORMS Journal on Optimization, Computational Optimization and Applications, NeurIPS, and AISTATS — and his research is supported by two National Science Foundation awards as PI. He received the 2025 Meritorious Service Award for exceptional refereeing from Mathematical Programming. He has graduated three Ph.D. students, and beyond research he is committed to outreach that brings optimization and machine learning to high-school students, including a hands-on TinyML program at the University of Arizona's Summer Engineering Academy.

Research Highlights

What I work on

Provably efficient first-order methods for structured optimization problems that are too large, too nested, or too distributed for classical approaches.

Bilevel Optimization

Computationally efficient, projection-free, and accelerated methods for nested optimization problems — from hyperparameter learning to planning and control. Supported by two NSF awards (2021–2024, 2025–2028).

Saddle Point, Minimax & Variational Inequalities

Primal-dual algorithms with line search, acceleration, variance reduction, and adaptive step sizes for convex-concave and nonconvex-concave saddle point problems, semi-infinite min-max problems, and misspecified variational inequalities — the backbone of robust and adversarial learning.

Distributed & Decentralized Optimization

Multi-agent methods for constrained optimization over static and time-varying networks — resource sharing, consensus, and asynchronous computation with rigorous convergence guarantees.

Optimization for Machine Learning

Stochastic and randomized first-order methods for learning at scale — with nine papers at NeurIPS and AISTATS — plus hands-on TinyML outreach that puts neural networks on microcontrollers.
