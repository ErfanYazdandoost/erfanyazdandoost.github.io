TinyML for High-School Students

A hands-on workshop — Decision-Making: Intro to Optimization, Neural Networks, and TinyML — where students go from "what is optimization?" to training a neural network and running it on a microcontroller they hold in their hands. No prerequisites. No cloud. Just curiosity, sticker cards, and a tiny camera.

The Journey

From "what is optimization?" to AI in your hand

The workshop builds one idea at a time — each concept motivated by a real decision problem before students ever touch code.

Optimize

What "best" means: choosing the fastest way to campus on a budget, staffing a dental clinic under uncertainty — then gradient descent as "walking downhill," and why the step size matters.

Classify

Binary classification through a healthcare lens: can a computer learn from labeled cells to tell benign from malignant? Loss functions as a score for "how wrong" a model is.

Go deep

Neural networks as layers of simple units — lines and colors first, then parts, then whole objects — trained by the very gradient descent students just learned.

Deploy tiny

TinyML: running models on devices with under 1 MB of memory and no GPU. Predictions happen on the board itself — no cloud required.

Hands-on

Two experiments, real hardware

Every student works with an Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense kit with an on-board camera — collecting data, training models in Python, and testing them live.

Experiment I · Binary Classification

⭐ Star vs. Circle

Students draw stars and circles on sticker cards, show them to the Arduino camera, and train a classifier with gradient descent on their own handmade dataset.

  • Collect and label 3–5 examples of each shape
  • Train and watch the loss curve fall in real time
  • Tune the learning rate (0.1 → 1 → 10) and see convergence — or divergence
  • Test on a neighbor's cards for an honest evaluation
Experiment II · Deep Learning + TinyML

✏️ Handwritten Digit Recognition

Students design their own neural network under true TinyML constraints, train it on MNIST, and deploy it to the microcontroller for a live accuracy showdown.

  • Architect a network: at most 4 hidden layers and 900 neurons
  • Choose an optimizer (Adam, SGD, RMSprop) and activation (ReLU, tanh, sigmoid)
  • Export the trained model and flash it onto the Arduino
  • Score it on printed digit cards — which digits fool the model?

Why TinyML?

The question that drives my research — how do we learn efficiently under tight resource constraints? — is exactly what makes TinyML click for students. When the entire model must fit in under a megabyte, every idea from optimization suddenly matters. Co-taught with Ph.D. student Cody Melcher at the University of Arizona's Summer Engineering Academy.
