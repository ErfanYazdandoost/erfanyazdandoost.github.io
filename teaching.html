Teaching

My teaching interests span optimization, operations research, machine learning, and probability — with a focus on connecting rigorous methods to real decision-making.

Courses

SIE 449 / 549 Undergraduate / Graduate

Optimization for Machine Learning

Optimization formulations and algorithms at the core of modern machine learning: gradient and stochastic gradient methods, acceleration, and the structured problems — such as minimax and bilevel formulations — behind training, regularization, and robust learning.

SIE 645 Graduate

Nonlinear Optimization

Theory and algorithms for nonlinear programming: optimality conditions, convexity, duality, and first- and second-order methods with convergence analysis — the foundation for research in continuous optimization.

SIE 265 / ENGR 265 Undergraduate

Engineering Management I

Principles of engineering management and decision-making: project planning, resource allocation, and quantitative tools for managing engineering systems and teams.

SIE 920 Doctoral

Dissertation Research

Supervision of doctoral research in large-scale optimization, saddle point problems, bilevel optimization, and optimization for machine learning. Meet the group →

Teaching philosophy

Optimization is best learned by moving between three views of the same idea: the geometric picture, the mathematical statement, and the computational experiment. In my courses, students implement the algorithms they analyze — observing in code how a step size changes convergence, or how structure in a problem can be exploited for speed — so theory becomes something they can see.

The same philosophy drives my outreach: in our TinyML program, high-school students discover gradient descent by training a neural network they then hold in the palm of their hand.
